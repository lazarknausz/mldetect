2 · Calibrate

On the frame, click and drag a line along something whose real length you know, then type that length. Drag the ends to adjust. Zoom the page in for precision: every pixel of error at the ends is error in the speed.

  • Road: draw along the lane, from the start of one dash to the start of the next (US highways: 12.19 m). Choose a stretch where cars are fully in the picture as they cross it, not at the frame's edge.
  • Vehicle: step to a frame where it moves across the view, then draw nose to tail. Measuring starts from that frame.

3 · Measure

How it works

Time: each frame is exactly 1 / FPS seconds after the previous one, so the gun steps through the video frame by frame.

Distance: the vehicle's pixel displacement × the calibration ratio (metres per pixel). The displacement comes from sub-pixel optical flow on the vehicle itself. Camera pans are subtracted.

Speed = distance / time, averaged with a least-squares fit over the last 0.5 s, because a single frame's pixel noise would swing the reading by ±10 km/h. A vehicle that crosses the whole road calibration line gets the exact trap reading: line length ÷ crossing time.

A calibration line is only exact at its own distance from the camera. That's why road calibrations measure vehicles in that stretch, and vehicle calibrations carry the line along on the vehicle, so its scale follows the vehicle as it comes closer, turns or the camera zooms.

Limits: one camera only sees motion across the picture. A vehicle driving straight at the camera barely moves on screen, so its speed can't be measured then. The reading is held while the vehicle is partly out of the frame.
